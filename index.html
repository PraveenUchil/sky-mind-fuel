01 / The story

WHAT'S
INSIDE
THE TANK?

Not petrol. Not an energy drink. Not a performance-enhancing supplement.

It's a metaphor for a practice: Sudarshan Kriya.

Sudarshan Kriya (SKY) is a structured rhythmic breathing practice taught through the Art of Living Foundation. It uses a systematic sequence of breathing patterns rather than simply taking deep breaths.

For me, carrying this oversized tank is a playful way to make something invisible visible: the importance of caring for the mind alongside the body.

03 / What the research suggests

BEYOND
THE HYPE.
INTO THE
EVIDENCE.

Researchers are exploring how SKY and related programs may affect stress, mood and psychological well-being. The findings are promising in some areas, but it's important to distinguish the evidence from performance claims.

01

STRESS & MOOD

SKY-based interventions have been associated with reductions in perceived stress and depressive symptoms.

02

EMOTIONAL WELL-BEING

Studies have reported improvements in positive emotions, mindfulness and overall mental health.

03

SOCIAL CONNECTION

Some research has found improvements in feelings of social connectedness. Focus benefits remain promising rather than established.

A NOTEWORTHY RANDOMIZED CONTROLLED TRIAL

In a 2020 Yale-affiliated study published in Frontiers in Psychiatry, 131 university students were assigned to a no-intervention control group or one of three eight-week programs: SKY Campus Happiness, Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction, or Foundations of Emotional Intelligence. Compared with the control group, the SKY Campus Happiness program showed significant benefits across six outcomes: depression, stress, mental health, mindfulness, positive affect and social connectedness. Important distinction: these findings concern the complete SKY Campus Happiness program, not Sudarshan Kriya breathing alone. The study was relatively small, and larger, longer-term studies are needed. This is not evidence of improved race times, endurance or VO₂ max. Read the research ↗
